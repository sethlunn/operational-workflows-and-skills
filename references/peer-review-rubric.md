# Peer Review Rubric

Use this reference when turning evidence about a colleague into a Zip FY26 performance peer review. It encodes Zip's "Writing an Effective Peer Review" standard plus the Situation-Behavior-Impact discipline so each entry stays specific, objective, balanced, and development-oriented.

## What The Review Must Answer

A Zip peer review has exactly two required entries. Write to these, not to a free-form essay.

- `Question 1 - Greatest strengths`
  2-3 strengths. Each strength needs a concrete example and the impact it had.
- `Question 2 - Where could they grow to increase their impact`
  1-2 growth opportunities. Each needs a concrete example and one actionable next step.

Context that shapes tone:

- Peer reviews are not anonymous. The feedback is shared with the Zipster's manager, not with the Zipster directly.
- The purpose is development and a fair, well-rounded assessment, so keep both entries useful to the manager and fair to the peer.

## Core Rule

Normalize each claim into four parts before it goes into a draft:

- one `value` anchor (which Zip value the behavior demonstrates)
- one short `behavior` (the specific, observable thing they did)
- one `impact` (what changed because of it, with a metric where one exists)
- one `evidence source` (Jira item, PR, review thread, or a user-supplied observation)

Keep the behavior concrete enough that the manager can picture the actual moment, not a personality summary.

## Situation-Behavior-Impact

Write every example as Situation -> Behavior -> Impact.

- `Situation` the project, PR, incident, or moment, named specifically.
- `Behavior` the observable action the peer took.
- `Impact` the outcome for the customer, the team, or the work, ideally quantified.

A statement that names only a trait ("strong communicator") is not yet a peer review. Attach the situation and the impact.

## Zip Values And Standards

Anchor strengths and growth to one of the four values. Use the standard verbs as the vocabulary.

- `Customer First`
  Advocate, Champion, Explore, Care. Decisions made in the customer's interest; understanding and improving the experience.
- `Own It`
  Commit, Prioritise, Evaluate, Take responsibility. Seeing work through, prioritising highest-impact work, managing risk, owning decisions and consequences.
- `Stronger Together`
  Collaborate, Respect, Enable, Empathise. Seeking diverse perspectives, sharing knowledge, opening doors for others, giving clear constructive feedback.
- `Change the Game`
  Adapt, Disrupt, Elevate, Excel. Embracing change and ambiguity, challenging the norm, taking calculated risks, setting and holding a high bar.

## Best Practices

From Zip's "Writing an Effective Peer Review":

- `Be Specific + Balanced` highlight strengths while also identifying real growth areas. Do not let either entry collapse to nothing.
- `Tie Feedback to Zip's Values` reference behaviors aligned with the values above.
- `Use Real Examples` cite concrete instances of impact and collaboration.
- `Be Objective` keep feedback constructive and focused on performance, not personality.
- `Encourage Development` for growth, suggest actionable improvements and skills to build, framed as a way to increase impact.

## Worked Example

Zip's own model answer, annotated by best practice:

- Specific + Balanced: "brings a strong, data-driven approach to decision making and consistently backs recommendations with evidence."
- Tie to Values: "their focus on clear analysis reflects Own It, making thoughtful, informed decisions."
- Real Example: "in our last project they developed a performance dashboard that helped identify key customer trends."
- Objective (growth): "at times their insights are dense and require interpretation, which limits how easily others can act on them."
- Encourage Development: "sharpening their storytelling and data visualization would make insights more accessible and improve communication with a broader audience."

Notice the growth point is about a skill that would raise impact, never about character.

## AI Impact

Zip's FY26 cycle explicitly values how Zipsters leverage AI and automation to improve outcomes. Surface it when there is real evidence, usually under `Change the Game` (Disrupt, Adapt, Excel) or as a personal-growth and learning-mindset signal.

- Distinguish "uses AI" from "uses AI to raise the bar": unblock others, accelerate delivery, improve quality, or lift the team's practices.
- Require a concrete instance: which tool or approach, what they built or accelerated, and the observed outcome.
- AI-usage claims and the reviewer's own observations of how the peer works come only from the user. Never infer or invent them from code or tickets.

## Evidence To Claim Mapping

- Jira epics, features, and stories -> scope, ownership, delivery, prioritisation, follow-through (Own It); customer-facing outcomes (Customer First).
- GitHub PRs and diffs -> quality bar, technical judgment, risk handling (Change the Game, Own It).
- Review threads (theirs and the user's) -> collaboration, responsiveness to feedback, enabling others, clarity (Stronger Together).
- User-supplied AI-usage notes and observations -> AI Impact, learning mindset, raising the team's bar.

Weight code- and Jira-supported evidence above impressions. Where the only support is a single thread or a vague recollection, flag it as low confidence to the user rather than writing it as fact.

## Dos And Don'ts

Don't:

- vague praise: "great to work with", "a joy on the team".
- character or trait judgments: "disorganized", "not a team player".
- unsupported opinion presented as fact.
- recency bias: writing only about the last sprint instead of the full FY26.
- growth framed as a personal flaw rather than an opportunity to increase impact.
- fabricated examples, metrics, or AI usage.

Do:

- name the situation, the observable behavior, and the impact.
- cite the specific PR, epic, or moment.
- anchor to a Zip value.
- for growth, give one concrete next step that would raise their impact.
- keep it concise: a few tight sentences per example, not a wall of text.

## Honesty Rules

- Claim only what the evidence supports.
- When evidence for a strength or growth area is thin, tell the user and ask for a concrete example before writing it.
- Never invent metrics, projects, quotes, or AI usage to fill an entry.
- If a peer's sample is too small for a confident growth point, say so rather than manufacturing a weakness.
